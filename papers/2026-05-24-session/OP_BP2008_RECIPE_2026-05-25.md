### 1.1 The misconception that broke method #4

In method #4 (`jax_su2_EE_BP2008_TRUE_v1_2026-05-25.py`), I treated U_1 and U_2 as TWO INDEPENDENT SU(2) configurations on L⁴ lattices, then attempted to define a "shared link" between them by setting `U_1[A_wrap, μ=τ] := U_2[A_wrap, μ=τ]`. This is **wrong** for two reasons :

1. **Phase space mismatch** : the conifold partition function `Z_2` is NOT an integral over `DU_1 × DU_2` with a constraint ; it is an integral over a SINGLE field `U` on a modified lattice topology where the temporal extent in A is `2T` and in Ā is `T`.

In my method #4, `U_1[A_wrap]` was unconstrained in S_conif but appeared in `S_W(U_1_mod)` evaluation, leading to `⟨S_W⟩_disc-ensemble` being anomalously HIGH on conif samples (because U_1[A_wrap] is Haar-distributed in disc samples but appears in disorder-frustrated plaquettes in conif evaluation).

### 1.2 The CORRECT BP2008 topology (BP2008b §II, Eq. (5), Fig. 1)

For Renyi-n EE of region A on a 4D Euclidean lattice :

**Geometry** : Take a Euclidean lattice of spatial size `N_r³` and temporal size `s · N_t` where `s = n` (the replica index) and `N_t = 1/(aT)`. Region A is a 3D slab of thickness `l` in the x-direction extending fully in y,z (`A = {0 ≤ x_0 < l}`). The complement Ā has periodic boundary in time direction with period `N_t` ; region A has periodic boundary with period `s · N_t`.

**Implementation** : on the array of sites `(x_0, x_1, x_2, t)`, identify lattice sites with periods `N_t` in time for spatial coordinates within Ā, otherwise (within A) the period is `s · N_t`. Then add links taking into account this periodical identification. The result is `Z_n([A, s, T]) = Z[A, s = n, T]`. From BP2008b Eq. (5) :

```
S[A] = lim_{T→0} ( lim_{s→1} ∂/∂s F[A, s, T] - F(T) )
```

where `F[A, s, T] = -ln Z[A, s, T]` and `F(T) = -ln Z(T)`.

**Fig. 1 of BP2008b** schematically shows the topology : the lattice in Ā has period `1/T`, the lattice in A has period `s/T`. The "branching points" are 3D-volumes at the time slices `t = k T` for `k = 0, 1, ..., s` — these are the cuts where the two pieces glue together. The boundary `∂A = ∂Ā` is the `(D-2) = 2`-dimensional surface of the slab A.

### 1.3 Rabenstein 2019 alternative geometry (1812.04279, §III, Fig. 2)

> Take a SINGLE lattice of spatial size `L³` and temporal size `2T` (for n=2). ## 2. Lattice action explicit formula

### 2.1 The action is STANDARD WILSON on the deformed lattice (BP2008b §II)

From BP2008b after Eq. (5) and Eq. (6) :

> Lattices with s cuts are obtained from `s N_t × N_r³` lattices with periodic boundary conditions in all space-like directions by identifying lattice sites with periods `N_t` and `s N_t` in time for spatial coordinates within A and B, respectively. These sites are then linked taking into account this periodical identification, and lattice gauge theory is defined on the obtained links. It turns out that lattices with such topology can be entirely mapped on `s D N_t × N_r³` hyper-cubic lattices so that all `s D N_t × N_r³` links and `s D(D-1)/2 N_t × N_r³` lattice plaquettes are used.

The action is the standard Wilson action :

```
S[U] = (β/N) Σ_p Re Tr(1 - U_p)
```

where the sum is over ALL plaquettes on the deformed lattice (i.e., the standard SU(N) Wilson sum), and the plaquette operator `U_p = U_μ(x) U_ν(x+μ) U_μ†(x+ν) U_ν†(x)` uses the deformed connectivity (which neighbor of `x` is `x+μ` depends on whether you're in A or Ā near the boundary surface t = 0 or t = T).

### 2.2 Rabenstein 2022 (2211.00425) explicit formula

The generalized partition function (Eq. (21)) :

```
Z(β, s, N_t, N_s, {n}) = ∫ DU exp{ (β/N) [ Σ_{□ ∈ C} Re Tr(U_□)
                                          + Σ_x̄ Σ_{ν=1}^{d-1} Σ_{r=1}^s Re Tr( U_{νd}^{(n_{x̄,ν})}(x̄, r·N_t - 1) ) ] }
```

where :
- `C` is the set of plaquettes whose value does NOT depend on whether their spatial location is in A or Ā (all plaquettes except the temporal ones that touch from below the time-slices `x_d = r · N_t` for `r = 1, ..., s`)
- `U_{νd}^{(0)}(x)` and `U_{νd}^{(1)}(x)` are the two possible values for the temporal plaquette (cf. Eq. (22)) :
  ```
  U_{νd}(x) = U_ν(x) U_d(x + ν̂) U_ν†(x + d̂) U_d†(x)
  ```
  depending on whether the plaquette is subject to temporal BC from outside (0, blue) or inside (1, red) region A.
- `n_{x̄, ν} ∈ {0, 1}` is a switch controlling whether each spatial link at the boundary is "inside" or "outside" A.

The α-integration parameter `α ∈ [0, 1]` interpolates between `n = 0` (= `Z_1^s`) and `n = 1` (= `Z_s(A)`).

### 2.3 No "cross-sheet plaquettes" with mixed U_1 and U_2

**Key contrast with method #4** : the BP2008 lattice does NOT have plaquettes that mix variables from different "sheets". There is ONE field `U` on the deformed lattice, and the action is the standard Wilson sum over the deformed plaquette structure. The Renyi-n information is in the TOPOLOGY (which neighbor is `x + τ̂`), not in some cross-sheet coupling.

## 3. Phase space and partition function ratio

### 3.1 SAME phase space, DIFFERENT actions

From Rabenstein 2022 Eq. (20) :

```
Z*_l(α) = ∫ DU exp(-(1-α) S_{G,l}[U] - α S_{G,l+1}[U])
```

This is THE crucial structural insight :

- `Z_c(l = 0, 2, N_t, N_s) = Z²(N_t, N_s)` (replica-disconnected = `Z₁²`)
- `Z_c(l, 2, N_t, N_s) = Z_2(A)` (replica-connected with slab width `l`)
- BOTH are integrals over the SAME field `U` over the SAME lattice topology (in Rabenstein's improved formulation), the only difference is which plaquettes are turned on by the boundary switch `{n}`.

Then by the **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** (Rabenstein 2019 Eq. (16)) :

```
F_2 - F_1 = -ln Z_2 + ln Z_1 = -∫_0^1 dα (∂/∂α) ln Z(α)
          = ∫_0^1 dα ⟨S_2[U] - S_1[U]⟩_{Z(α)}
```

### 3.2 BAR is the WRONG technique here

BAR (Bennett 1976) requires that the two ensembles `P_1(U) ∝ exp(-S_1)` and `P_2(U) ∝ exp(-S_2)` have non-negligible overlap in phase space. The Rindlisbacher 2022 paper explicitly states (§3) :

> When trying to determine the free energy difference [...] by direct measurements, [...] one will be facing a severe overlap problem : link-variable configurations that (significantly) contribute to the partition function Z_c(l=0, 2, N_t, N_s) play essentially no role for the partition function Z_c(l, 2, N_t, N_s) (and vice versa); a reliable estimate on the log of the observable [...] would therefore require exponentially large statistics.

This is **precisely** the sign/overlap problem that killed methods 1, 2, 3, 4.

### 3.3 The correct approach : α-integration with cubic-spline interpolation

Rabenstein 2019 §III, Eq. (17)-(20) :

1. Generate gauge configs with interpolating action `S_int = (1-α) S_l[U] + α S_{l+1}[U]` for several values of α ∈ {0, 0.1, ..., 1.0}.
2. Measure `⟨S_{l+1} - S_l⟩_α` for each α.
3. Integrate over α with cubic-spline interpolation to get the **derivative** :
   ```
   ∂S^(2)/∂l |_{l-a/2} = ∫_0^1 dα ⟨S_{l+1} - S_l⟩_α
   ```
4. Compute the entropic C-function (Eq. (20)) :
   ```
   C(l - a/2) = (l - a/2)³ / (a L²) · ∂S^(2)/∂l |_{l-a/2}
   ```

### 3.4 The 2022 Wang-Landau improvement (Rindlisbacher 2211.00425)

For larger systems where even the α-integration has too-large a free-energy barrier, use Wang-Landau sampling on the generalized PF Eq. (25) to determine `F_{N_K} - F_0 = log Z_{N_K} - log Z_0` directly. This eliminates the integration over α entirely.

> We are using a pseudo-heatbath algorithm to update the gauge configurations. Doing this we have to calculate the closing plaquettes for the link which we want to update. In other words : the per-link update is the **standard** SU(N) heat-bath/Cabbibo-Marinari, BUT when computing the staple sum, one must use the **deformed connectivity** — for sites near the boundary `t = 0` of A and Ā, the "neighbor at `t + 1`" is different in the two regions.

## 5. EE estimator (formula + derivation)

### 5.1 BP2008b approach : measure dS/dl directly

From BP2008b Eq. (7)-(9), they measure :

```
∂S/∂l = ∂S_f/∂l = lim_{T→0} lim_{s→1} (∂/∂l)(∂/∂s) F[A, s, T]
```

approximated by finite differences for `s=2` cuts :

```
(1/|∂A|) (∂/∂l) S_f(l + a/2) ≈ [F(l+a, 2, T) - F(l, 2, T)] / (a |∂A|)
```

(Eq. (9) of BP2008b). The free-energy difference `F(l+a, 2, T) - F(l, 2, T)` is computed via Fodor-Endrödi α-integration of an interpolating partition function (BP2008b Appendix B, Eq. (B1)-(B3)).

### 5.2 Rabenstein 2019 unified formula

`S^(q)` is approximated by the lattice formula `S^(q) = (1/(q-1))[F[A,q,T] - q F[T]]` (Eq. (14) of Rabenstein 2019). For `q = 2` :

```
S^(2) = F[A, 2, T] - 2 F[T] = -log(Z_2(A) / Z²(T))
```

The derivative is computed by central finite difference Eq. (15) :

```
(∂/∂l) S^(2)(l - a/2) ≈ [F(l-a, 2, T) - F(l, 2, T)] / a
```

The entropic C-function (Eq. (20)) :

```
C(l - a/2) = (l - a/2)³ / (a L²) · ∂S^(2)/∂l |_{l-a/2}
```

### 5.3 Is `S_2 = -log(Z_2/Z_1²)` directly measurable?

**NO**, not in BP2008b nor Rabenstein 2019 nor Rindlisbacher 2022. The reason is that `Z_2` and `Z_1²` are BOTH UV-divergent quantities ; their ratio has divergences that scale as `|∂A|/a²` plus a finite UV-divergent l-independent piece. What is physically meaningful is the DERIVATIVE `∂S/∂l`, which cancels these divergences.

**Hence the correct observable is the SLOPE of `S_2` with `l`, not the absolute value.** This is a fundamental reorientation compared to my naive Method #4 attempt to compute `S_2` directly.

### 5.4 The C-function: what to fit and check

For the Attempt B test `c =? 1/4`, the appropriate quantity is :

```
C_BH_predicted(l) = (l³ / 2 L²) · (1/4) · dA/dl  for l in the regime where area law dominates
```

For a 3D slab in 4D, `|∂A| = 2 L²` (two parallel planes), and `S_EE(l) ≈ c · 2 L² + finite + power-law correction`. So `∂S_EE/∂l ≈ 0` for the leading area-law piece, and the C-function picks up only the FINITE non-trivial scaling. **The naive expectation `c = S_2/|∂A| = 1/4` does NOT translate to `C(l) = 1/4` directly.** What it does predict is the proportionality coefficient between the lattice spacing `a`-dependent UV-divergent part and `|∂A|/a²`, NOT the entropic C-function.

**This is a key conceptual update for the Attempt B test : the lattice does NOT cleanly extract `c` ; it extracts a C-function whose interpretation is more subtle.**

## 6. Region A and boundary identification

### 6.1 Region A geometry

**Both BP2008b and Rabenstein 2019** use a 3D slab :

```
A = {x ∈ Z^3 : 0 ≤ x_0 < l} × {t = arbitrary}
```

i.e., A is the FULL 3D-region at all times within a slab in the `x_0`-direction of thickness `l`, extending to the periodic spatial size in `x_1, x_2`.

### 6.2 Boundary ∂A

The "entangling surface" `∂A = ∂Ā` is :

- BP2008b §II : "the three-volumes B at the time slices t = k T, k = 0, 1, ..., s, which have a common boundary ∂A = ∂B" — i.e., for s = 2, two 3-volumes glued along the (D-2) = 2-dimensional boundary of A.
- Rabenstein 2019 §II : "due to the periodic boundary conditions in space directions, the corresponding entangling surface consists of two parallel planes and thus has neither extrinsic curvature nor sharp corners".

So `|∂A| = 2 L²` for periodic BC (two parallel x_1, x_2-planes at `x_0 = 0` and `x_0 = l`).

### 6.3 NOT a spatial-only region

The slab A is a region of SPACE-TIME, not of pure space. The replica trick glues `s = n` copies of the lattice along A (and identifies the temporal boundaries inside Ā). This is the Calabrese-Cardy 2004 replica trick adapted to 4D lattice gauge theory.

### 6.4 Consistency with my Attempt B hypothesis

My hypothesis was : `c = S_2 / |∂A| = 1/4` for SU(2), where |∂A| = 2 L² is the 2-plane area. This is a **per-area** coefficient (units : pure number for `S_2` in nats and `|∂A|` in lattice units). To compare with Rabenstein's C-function (Eq. (7)) :

```
C(l) = (l³ / 2 L²) · ∂S_EE/∂l
```

these are DIFFERENT quantities. `S_EE/|∂A|` is the LEADING area-law term. `C(l)` is the SUB-LEADING finite scaling coefficient. **My Attempt B should target the LEADING coefficient, NOT the C-function.**

This requires extracting `S_EE` itself, not just its derivative — and BP2008b explicitly says this is "very difficult" because of UV divergences ; they only access derivatives reliably.

### 7.4 What does `C(SU(2)) ≈ 0.054` mean for Attempt B `c = 1/4`?

The C-function `C(l) = (l³/2L²) · ∂S_EE/∂l` is **NOT** the area-law coefficient. The area-law coefficient is the value of `S_EE/|∂A|` extrapolated to the UV. To compare with `c = 1/4`, one would need to :

1. Integrate `∂S_EE/∂l` over `l` to get `S_EE(l)` (with the integration constant fixed by some renormalization)
2. Extract the leading area-law term (most divergent term in `1/a`)
3. Compare with `1/4`

Naïvely, the UV-divergent area-law term is `S_UV/|∂A| = κ · Λ_UV² = κ · a⁻²` (BP2008b §I), where `κ` is the coefficient I want to test. BP2008b §III subtracts this divergence by working with `s = 2` and `s = 3` differences, finding the divergent piece does not depend on `l` (= conservation of area-law coefficient). The COEFFICIENT `κ` itself is NOT directly tabulated in BP2008b.

**Status of Attempt B test on lattice** :

- Rabenstein 2019 measures `C(l)` for SU(2), NOT `κ`. To extract `κ`, one would need a separate calculation : integrate the divergent piece `S_UV(l)` independently, then fit it to `κ · a⁻² · |∂A|`.
- Such a calculation is in BP2008b §III (Fig. 3) but the result is not given as a single number for `κ`. Instead they show "the average action excess practically does not depend on the lattice spacing" — meaning `κ Λ²_UV` is L-stable, but the numerical value of `κ` is not extracted cleanly.
- To my knowledge, **NO lattice study to date has cleanly extracted the universal area-law coefficient `κ`** for SU(2) Yang-Mills in 4D. This is a genuinely open numerical question.

## 8. Implementation recommendations for JAX

### 8.1 Stop trying BAR/swap-based methods

The 4 failed approaches all attempted to compute `S_2 = -log(Z_2/Z_1²)` directly via BAR or swap-based estimators. **This is doomed to fail** because of the severe overlap problem (Rindlisbacher 2022 §3) and the UV-divergent nature of the ratio.

### 8.2 Implement Rabenstein 2019 α-integration

The cleanest path is :

1. **Deformed lattice array** : on a `(L, L, L, 2T)` array, build a custom topology where for sites `x_0 ∈ Ā` (i.e., `x_0 ≥ l`), the temporal neighbor of site `(x, t=T-1)` is `(x, t=0)`, and the temporal neighbor of site `(x, t=2T-1)` is `(x, t=T)` (so `Ā` has period T). For sites `x_0 ∈ A` (`x_0 < l`), the temporal neighbor of site `(x, t=2T-1)` is `(x, t=0)` (period 2T).

2. **Indexing functions** : write `next_t(x_0, t)` that returns the correct temporal neighbor depending on `x_0`. This is a JIT-able lookup table of shape `(L, 2T) → (L, 2T)`.

3. **Wilson action with deformed connectivity** : the temporal plaquettes `P_{μ4}(x, t)` use this deformed connectivity.

4. **Pseudo-heatbath** : standard SU(2) heatbath on each link, but the staple sum uses the deformed connectivity.

5. **Generate the interpolating action** : `S_int = (1-α) S_l[U] + α S_{l+1}[U]` where `S_l` is the action on a lattice with slab width `l` and `S_{l+1}` is on a lattice with slab width `l+1`. Use Rindlisbacher 2022 Eq. (21) for the explicit form.

6. **Measure `⟨S_{l+1} - S_l⟩_α`** for α ∈ {0, 0.1, ..., 1.0}.

7. **Integrate over α** by cubic-spline interpolation. Result : `∂S^(2)/∂l |_{l+1/2}`.

8. **Compute C(l)** via Rabenstein 2019 Eq. (20).

9. **For Attempt B `c = 1/4` test** : also accumulate the UV-divergent area-law piece by integrating `S_EE(l)` directly from the derivative, with a reference subtraction at some fixed `l_0`. This requires multiple slab widths `l = 1, 2, 3, ..., L/2`.

### 8.3 Cheaper alternative : Rindlisbacher 2022 Wang-Landau

Rindlisbacher 2022 proposes a single Wang-Landau run instead of multiple α-integration points. This avoids the cubic-spline interpolation overhead but requires implementing Wang-Landau sampling (more complex than vanilla α-integration).

### 8.4 Computational cost estimate

Rabenstein 2019 for SU(2) on L=16, T=16 :

- 11 α-values per `l`, with O(2·10⁶) configs per α → ~2·10⁷ configs per `l`
- 6 values of `l` (from 2 to 7 lattice spacings) → ~1.2·10⁸ total configs
- 100 sweep decorrelation, pseudo-heatbath sweep ~ 10⁻⁴ sec on RTX 3090 for L=16

## 9. Cross-checks against Donnelly, Velytsky, Aoki, Rabenstein

### 9.1 Velytsky 2008 (0801.4111) : Migdal-Kadanoff approach

**Method** : NOT Monte Carlo. Uses approximate RG flow of character expansion coefficients `c_r` under Migdal-Kadanoff decimation.

**Key result** : for SU(2) 3+1D, finds a non-analytic transition in the RG flow of `c_r` at `l*_c / l_c ∈ (1.56, 1.66)` (where `l_c = 1/T_c` is the confinement scale). Approximate, not directly comparable to Rabenstein 2019.

**Implementation viability for JAX** : not really — Migdal-Kadanoff is character-space iteration, not gauge-config Monte Carlo. Different paradigm.

### 9.2 Donnelly 2012 (1109.0036) : 3-term decomposition

**Formula (Donnelly 2012 Eq. (32))** :

```
S(ρ_A) = H(p(R_∂)) + ⟨Σ_{l ∈ L_∂A} ln dim(r_l)⟩ + ⟨S(ρ_A(R_∂))⟩
```

where :
- `R_∂` is the assignment of irreducible representations to boundary links
- `p(R_∂)` is the probability distribution over boundary representations
- `H(p)` is the Shannon entropy
- `⟨Σ ln dim(r_l)⟩` is the weighted average of `ln(2j+1)` for SU(2) boundary links
- `⟨S(ρ_A(R_∂))⟩` is the bulk-correlation contribution

**For SU(2)** : `dim(r) = 2j + 1`, so the boundary term is `n · ⟨ln(2j+1)⟩` where `n` is the number of boundary links.

**Implementation viability for JAX** : YES, this is the cleanest THEORETICAL formula. But measuring `p(R_∂)` on the lattice requires extracting boundary-link character distributions, which is more involved than the Rabenstein 2019 α-integration. Not directly competitive for getting the area-law coefficient.

### 9.3 Donnelly-Wall 2014 (1412.1895) : Edge modes for Maxwell

**Method** : Treats electromagnetic field with "edge modes" living on the entangling surface, regularized by a brick wall. Derives `Z_edge` and `Z_contact` as separate factors.

**For non-abelian SU(2)** : the paper explicitly says (footnote and discussion) that "Edge modes also occur in nonabelian lattice Yang-Mills theory ; it would be interesting to take a similar continuum limit for that theory". This is a CONJECTURE for non-abelian, not a derivation.

**Implementation viability for JAX** : not directly. Conceptual / continuum paper, not a lattice algorithm.

### 9.4 Aoki-Iritani-Nozaki-Numasawa-Shiba-Tasaki 2015 (1502.04267) : Extended Hilbert space definition

**Method** : DEFINES the EE in lattice gauge theory via extended Hilbert space, derives the replica formula `Z_n` as a 4D path integral with specific boundary conditions (Eq. (2.21)-(2.23)) :

```
Z_n = ∏_{i=1}^n ∫ DU_i  ρ_V^T(U_1; U_2) ρ_V^T(U_2; U_3) ... ρ_V^T(U_n; U_1)
```

and notes (Eq. (2.24)) that the resulting path integral is **invariant under local gauge transformation g in d+1 dimensions with period 2N_T at the boundary** — NOT 2nN_T — which constrains the replica geometry.

**Key insight from Aoki 2015** : their formulation is **gauge-invariant by construction** (Eq. (2.7)) and uses the **gauge-non-invariant extended Hilbert space of link variables** for the trace. This is the **theoretical foundation** for the lattice methods used by BP2008b and Rabenstein 2019.

**Implementation viability** : conceptually identical to Rabenstein 2019. The lattice topology and action are the same.

### 9.5 Itou-Aoki-Nagata 2016 (1608.08727) : 1+1D exact

**For 1+1D SU(N) gauge theory** they derive (Eq. (3.8)) :

```
S_R(A) = 2ℓ · log d_R
```

where `ℓ` is the number of disjoint components of A (in 1D, A is a disjoint union of intervals, each interval has 2 endpoints = 2 boundary points). For SU(2) fundamental representation `R = 1/2`, `d_R = 2`, so `S_{1/2}(A) = 2ℓ · log 2`.

**For 4D** : their formula doesn't directly extend, but it confirms that in 1+1D the EE is **purely edge-mode** (Donnelly 2012 first term = `2ℓ ln 2` per boundary endpoint pair for SU(2)).

**Implementation viability** : not directly relevant for 4D, but a useful sanity check : the SU(2) edge-mode coefficient should appear as `log 2 ≈ 0.693` per boundary spin-1/2 link.

### 9.6 Rabenstein-Bodendorfer-Buividovich-Schäfer 2019 (1812.04279) : BEST SU(2) 4D recipe

**This is the gold standard.** Everything in §1-8 above follows their recipe.

**SU(2) 4D result** : C-function ≈ 0.054 in the UV regime.

### 9.7 Rindlisbacher-Jokela-Pönni-Rummukainen-Salami 2022 (2211.00425) : Wang-Landau improvement

**Improvement** : single-run Wang-Landau in place of multi-α-integration. Reduces statistical error and avoids overlap problem.

**Implementation viability for JAX** : Yes, but more complex than vanilla α-integration. Recommended after the vanilla α-integration is working.
