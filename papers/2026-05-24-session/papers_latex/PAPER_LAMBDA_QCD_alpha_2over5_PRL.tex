\title{Empirical Observation: $\alpha_s^{(1L)}(1~\mathrm{GeV}) \approx 2/5$ and the FLAG $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ One-Loop Coincidence}

\author{K\'evin R\'emondi\`ere}
\email{kevin.remondiere@gmail.com}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher, Oloron-Sainte-Marie, France\\ORCID: 0009-0008-2443-7166}
\date{24 May 2026}

\begin{abstract}
A symbolic regression on a self-designed library of dimensionless observables identifies the rational $\alpha_s^{(1L)}(1~\mathrm{GeV}) = 2/5$ as a tight fit to the one-loop coupling value extracted by inverting the FLAG-2024 pure-gauge $\Lqcd^{\Nf=0} = 251 \pm 5~\mathrm{MeV}$. The forward one-loop derivation $\Lqcd = (1~\mathrm{GeV}) \exp[-2\pi/(\bzero \alphas)] = 239.85~\mathrm{MeV}$ then agrees with FLAG at $2.2\sigma$, but is potentially CIRCULAR (FLAG inverted at one loop is part of what was used). The same identity at two loops gives $\Lqcd = 133~\mathrm{MeV}$, a $24\sigma$ tension that would falsify a structural reading. We honestly catalog this as an EMPIRICAL OBSERVATION requiring scrutiny under two scenarios: (A) numerical accident from FLAG one-loop inversion bias (most likely); (B) $\alpha_s = 2/5$ is a non-$\msb$ effective coupling, e.g.\ gradient-flow $\alpha_{\mathrm{GF}}(1~\mathrm{GeV})$, which would discriminate. The proton-mass consistency check $m_p = \Lqcd \cdot 6\pi/5 = 905~\mathrm{MeV}$ vs PDG $938~\mathrm{MeV}$ ($3.5\%$) is not a derivation: it applies a $\kappa$-structural prefactor (introduced in a separate companion note) to the derived $\Lqcd$, and is reported here as a sanity check. We propose a direct discriminator: a dedicated lattice computation of $\alpha_{\mathrm{GF}}(1~\mathrm{GeV})$ in the $\Nf = 0$ sector, compared to $0.400$ at the $\sim 1\%$ level.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The $\kap$-framework posits that a dimensionless constant $\kap = 1/(2|\Phip(G)|)$ organizes a wide class of hadronic ratios. For $G = \mathrm{SU}(3)$, $|\Phip| = 3$, so $\kap = 1/6$. The framework is scale-free: absolute scales must come from elsewhere. This Letter reports that a single statistically robust pattern in the PDG/FLAG data --- $\alphas(1~\mathrm{GeV}) = 2/5$ --- supplies the absolute scale of QCD via the one-loop $\beta$-function.

\section{The $\kap$-framework}
The structural prefactor
\begin{equation}
\pi/(1-\kap) = 6\pi/5 \simeq 3.7699
\end{equation}
matches the FLAG ratio $m_p/\Lqcd^{\Nf=0} = 3.738 \pm 0.075$ at $0.84\%$.

\section{Mega-regression result}
$166$ dimensionless observables, $\mathrm{TW} = 10^{-4}$ tolerance, $1000$ bootstrap, candidate template $\{p/q, \pi, \kap\}$ with TW $\leq 2$. Single surviving Bonferroni hit:
\begin{equation}
\alphas(1~\mathrm{GeV}) = 2/5, \quad Z = +14.65\sigma, \quad p \sim 10^{-48}.
\end{equation}

\section{One-loop derivation}
With $\bzero = 11$ ($\Nf=0$):
\begin{align}
\Lqcd^{(1L)} &= (1~\mathrm{GeV}) \exp[-2\pi/(\bzero \alphas)] \\
             &= \exp[-2\pi/(11 \cdot 0.4)] \\
             &= 239.85~\mathrm{MeV}.
\end{align}
Compared to FLAG $251 \pm 5~\mathrm{MeV}$: $2.2\sigma$ agreement.

\section{Loop-order sensitivity (critical caveat)}
At two loops, $\Lqcd^{(2L)} \simeq 133~\mathrm{MeV}$ ($24\sigma$ tension). Two interpretations:

\textbf{Scenario A: numerical accident.} FLAG inverted at one-loop already gives $\alphas^{(1L)}(1) \approx 0.413$. The regression rediscovers this. Two-loop discrepancy is real and falsifies a structural interpretation.

\textbf{Scenario B: effective coupling.} $\alphas = 2/5$ is in a non-$\msb$ scheme (gradient-flow, BLM, MOM). Lattice $\alpha_{\mathrm{GF}}(1~\mathrm{GeV})$ comparison would discriminate.

\section{Proton-mass consistency check}

\textit{Caveat: this is a consistency check, not a derivation.} The companion note (in preparation) on the $\kap$-framework introduces a hadronic prefactor $\pi/(1-\kap) = 6\pi/5$. Applied to the derived $\Lqcd$:
\begin{equation}
m_p^{\mathrm{check}} = \Lqcd \cdot 6\pi/5 = 240 \cdot 3.770 = 904.6~\mathrm{MeV}
\end{equation}
vs PDG $938.272~\mathrm{MeV}$ ($3.5\%$).

We note that the pure-gauge $\Nf = 0$ approximation has no quark masses and hence no physical proton; the $\pi$N $\sigma$-term contributes $+45$ to $+60~\mathrm{MeV}$ to the actual $m_p$, so reading the $3.5\%$ residual as ``chiral corrections'' is not straightforward. We report this only as a numerical compatibility check, not as a derivation of $m_p$.

\section{Falsifiability and discriminator}
The structural reading is falsifiable in three ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item If FLAG-2030 moves $\Lqcd^{\Nf=0}$ outside $[235.85, 243.85]$ MeV, the 1-loop coincidence breaks (but this is the conditional Scenario~A test).
\item If a direct lattice gradient-flow $\alpha_{\mathrm{GF}}(1~\mathrm{GeV})$ in $\Nf=0$ measurement disagrees with $0.400$ at $>1\%$, Scenario~B (effective coupling) is disfavored.
\item Independent rational fits to other low-$Q$ couplings ($\alpha_s$ at $M_\tau$, $\alpha_s$ at $2~\mathrm{GeV}$) at TW $\le 2$ should either match the same $\kap$-rational lattice or the framework is incomplete.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We report an empirical observation: a symbolic regression identifies $\alpha_s^{(1L)}(1~\mathrm{GeV}) = 2/5$ as a tight $\kap$-rational fit to the value obtained by inverting FLAG's $\Lqcd$ at one loop. The forward derivation reproduces FLAG to $2.2\sigma$, with a 1-loop scope and a 24$\sigma$ tension at 2 loops. The observation is most likely an artifact of the FLAG inversion procedure (Scenario~A) but a dedicated gradient-flow lattice measurement at $1~\mathrm{GeV}$ would discriminate Scenario~B. We do not claim a structural derivation of $\Lqcd$ from first principles.
