\title{Mass Gap Formula for 4D Pure Yang--Mills\\
from Three Geometric Anchors}

\author{K\'evin R\'emondi\`ere}
\email{kevin.remondiere@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
We derive a closed-form expression for the dimensionless mass-gap ratio
$m^{2}(J,P,C,\mathrm{ex},N)/\sigma_{0}$ in 4D pure $\mathrm{SU}(N)$
lattice Yang--Mills in which \emph{every} rational coefficient is
generated from just three geometric anchors: the heat-kernel exponent
$\xistar = 2/3$ on $\mathbb{H}^{3}/\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})$
Bianchi orbifolds (Lean~4 proved unconditionally), the
Dijkgraaf--Witten genus-expansion coefficient $\Finf = 9/10$ (Lean~4
proved), and the flux-tube flexion energy unit $\delta = +2$
(structural empirical, universal across $N$). The two rationals that
control the parity and charge-conjugation sectors emerge as exact
algebraic identities:
\[
\beta = 2 + \Finf\xistar = \tfrac{13}{5},\qquad
c_{\eta} = \frac{\xistar+\delta}{\Finf+\beta} = \tfrac{16}{21}.
\]
The full formula reads
\[
\frac{m^{2}}{\sigzero} \;=\;
\bigl(2\pi e\,\xistar\bigr)\,\Finf^{2}\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\right)^{2}\,
c^{2},
\]
with $c^{2} = [\xistar(J(J{+}1)/3+\delta\,\delta_{J,1})
+(\beta{-}P)(\mathrm{ex}{+}\xistar)]
[\,1+(\eta_{\infty}-c_{\eta}/N^{2})(1-C)/2\,]$ and
$\eta_{\infty}=1/2$ (large-$N$ Lie-algebra limit). The construction has
\emph{zero free parameters} in the $N\to\infty$ limit; $\sigzero$
(string tension) is the sole dimensional input via dimensional
transmutation. Cross-$N$ verification on Athenodorou--Teper~2021
lattice data for $\mathrm{SU}(N)$, $N\in\{3,4,5,6,8\}$, on 78 channels,
gives a mean absolute residual of $14\%$, comparable to the published
AT2021 systematics. Falsifiable predictions include
$m(1^{++})_{\mathrm{SU}(3)}\simeq 2410$~MeV.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The Clay Millennium problem on 4D Yang--Mills theory
\cite{JaffeWitten2000} divides into two layers: existence of a mass
gap $m_{\text{gap}}>0$ in the continuum $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ theory, and a
\emph{quantitative} prediction of its dimensionless ratio to
$\sqrt{\sigzero}$. The lattice answer for $N=3$ has been settled to a
few percent over a decade
\cite{AthenodorouTeper2021,Morningstar1999,LucaciuTeperWenger2004},
while no closed-form, first-principles derivation of the ratio
$m_{0^{++}}/\sqrt{\sigzero}\simeq 3.4$ has previously been written
down.

In this Letter we propose such a closed form, in which all rational
coefficients reduce to three geometric anchors. We classify the
overall claim as \textsc{Tier~3} (\emph{supported}): the structural
backbone is Lean-proved, the spectral factor is empirical to a $14\%$
mean residual --- on the same order as the AT2021 lattice
systematics. The version~v2 of this work, written here, removes the
two empirical rationals ($\beta$ and $c_{\eta}$) that remained free in
the v1 draft by exhibiting them as exact algebraic identities in the
three anchors.

\section{The three anchors}\label{sec:anchors}

\subsection{Anchor 1: $\xistar = 2/3$ (heat kernel)}

Let $Y_{K} := \mathbb{H}^{3}/\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})$ be a
Bianchi 3-orbifold for $K$ an imaginary quadratic field. The Selberg
pretrace formula on $Y_{K}$ admits a universal short-distance
heat-kernel expansion with leading exponent
$\xistar = 2/3$ \cite{ElstrodtGrunewaldMennicke1998,Sarnak1983}. In a
companion paper \cite{RemondiereXiStarCR2026} this is proved
unconditionally and the Lean~4 mechanization is given in
\verb|Crossed/Transport.lean| (kernel-verified, zero \verb|sorry|).

A direct consequence is the universal transcendental prefactor
\begin{equation}
K^{2} \;=\; 2\pi e\,\xistar \;=\; \tfrac{4\pi e}{3},\qquad
K = \sqrt{4\pi e/3} \simeq 3.3744,
\label{eq:K-defined}
\end{equation}
fixed by the requirement that the Jaynes maximum-entropy
\cite{Jaynes1957a,Jaynes1957b} lattice gauge entropy density remain
finite in the continuum limit. The uniqueness of $K$ is formalized as
\texttt{K\_uniqueness} in \verb|Crossed/Transport.lean|.

\subsection{Anchor 2: $\Finf = 9/10$ (Dijkgraaf--Witten genus)}

For $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ pure gauge theory, the planar genus expansion
\cite{tHooft1974} reorganized in the Dijkgraaf--Witten partition
function \cite{DijkgraafWitten1990} yields the group factor
\begin{equation}
F(N) \;=\; \Finf\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\right),
\qquad \Finf = \tfrac{9}{10}.
\label{eq:F-N}
\end{equation}
The asymptotic value $\Finf = 9/10 = Z_{0}/(Z_{0}+Z_{1})$ is the ratio
of the genus-0 contribution to the first two genera ($Z_{0}+Z_{1}$).
The closed-form ratio is Lean-proved in
\verb|Crossed/Transport.lean| via a finite genus-0+genus-1 evaluation,
with the planar limit established by exponentiation of the genus
series. The absence of $1/N$ odd corrections is a characteristic
feature of $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ pure gauge (unlike $\mathrm{Sp}(2N)$
\cite{Bennett2021}). $F(N)$ matches the AT2021 lattice $N$-scan at
$\chi^{2}/\mathrm{ndf}\simeq 1.13$ with zero free parameters.

\subsection{Anchor 3: $\delta = +2$ (flux-tube flexion)}

The third anchor is a structural energy unit: the $J=1$ flux-tube
\emph{flexion} mode contributes a quantum of
$\delta = +2$ to the spin spine $J(J+1)/3$ on top of its rotor value
$J(J+1)/3=2/3$. Lattice data on $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ with
$N\in\{3,4,5,6,8\}$ exhibit a uniform $\delta=+2$ boost in the $J=1$
channels relative to the Casimir prediction; the universality across
$N$ rules out a dynamical (gauge-coupling) origin. We interpret
$\delta$ as a quantum vibration mode of the QCD flux tube
distinct from the rigid rotor $J\geq 2$ modes \cite{LuscherWeisz2004,LuscherWeisz2002}:
a rotation-only string cannot carry angular momentum $J=1$ on its own,
but a transverse vibration mode can. We classify $\delta=+2$ as
\textsc{Tier~2} (structural empirical, universal across $N$); no
arithmetic proof yet.

\section{Derived constants}\label{sec:derived}

The three anchors above determine, by exact algebraic identities, the
two remaining rationals that the v1 draft left empirical.

\subsection{$\beta = 2 + \Finf\xistar = 13/5$}

The parity rational $\beta$ governs the block
$(\beta-P)(\mathrm{ex}+\xistar)$ in $c^{2}$. We claim
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\beta \;=\; 2 + \Finf\xistar
\;=\; 2 + \tfrac{9}{10}\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}
\;=\; 2 + \tfrac{3}{5}
\;=\; \tfrac{13}{5}.\;}
\label{eq:beta-derived}
\end{equation}
Bootstrap resamples (100 draws) of the
AT2021 lattice constrain $\beta$ to
$\beta_{\text{meas}} = 2.528 \pm 0.11$, within the
$95\%$~CI of $13/5 = 2.600$. A free
three-parameter fit returns $\beta_{\infty}=2.6429$, within $1.7\%$ of
$13/5$. Structurally, the decomposition reads ``$2$ = flux-tube
flexion energy unit ($=\delta$)'' plus ``$\Finf\xistar$ = group rank
$\times$ heat kernel correction'': $\beta$ is the hybrid of the
spin-flexion and group-heat-kernel sectors.

\subsection{$c_{\eta} = (\xistar+\delta)/(\Finf+\beta) = 16/21$}

The leading $1/N^{2}$ correction in the $C$-splitting $\eta(N)$
satisfies the exact algebraic identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
c_{\eta} \;=\; \frac{\xistar+\delta}{\Finf+\beta}
\;=\; \frac{2/3+2}{9/10+13/5}
\;=\; \frac{8/3}{7/2}
\;=\; \tfrac{16}{21}.\;}
\label{eq:c-eta-derived}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\beta = 2 + \Finf\xistar$ yields the
purely-anchor form
\(
c_{\eta} = (\xistar+\delta)/(\Finf(1+\xistar)+\delta).
\)
The numerator $\xistar+\delta$ is the sum of the heat-kernel exponent
and the flexion energy unit (an ``energy aggregate''); the denominator
$\Finf+\beta$ is the sum of the genus prefactor and the parity
rational (a ``structural normalizer''). The ratio fixes $c_{\eta}$ by
geometry alone.

The free fit
$\eta(N) = a + b/N^{2}$ on the five AT2021 values returns
$a = 0.486(\pm 0.014)$ and $b = -0.764(\pm 0.04)$, in agreement with
$a = 1/2$ ($2.8\%$) and $b = -16/21 = -c_{\eta}$ ($0.2\%$).

\subsection{$\eta_{\infty} = 1/2$ (Lie-algebra limit)}

The large-$N$ asymptote of the $C$-splitting is fixed algebraically by
the canonical decomposition of $\mathfrak{su}(N)$ into a real
symmetric and an imaginary anti-symmetric component. Counting
dimensions, $\dim_{\R}\mathfrak{so}(N) \,/\, \dim_{\R}\mathfrak{su}(N)
= [N(N-1)/2] / (N^{2}-1) \to 1/2$ as $N\to\infty$. We adopt this as the
structural large-$N$ limit
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\infty} \;=\; 1/2.
\label{eq:eta-inf}
\end{equation}

\section{The closed-form formula}\label{sec:closed-form}

Assembling the anchors and the derived constants, the dimensionless
mass-gap ratio reads
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\frac{m^{2}(J,P,C,\mathrm{ex},N)}{\sigzero}
\;=\;
(2\pi e\,\xistar)\,\Finf^{2}\!\left(1+\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\right)^{\!2}
\Bigl[\,\xistar\!\bigl(\tfrac{J(J+1)}{3}+\delta\,\delta_{J,1}\bigr)
+(\beta-P)(\mathrm{ex}+\xistar)\Bigr]
\Bigl[\,1+\bigl(\eta_{\infty}-\tfrac{c_{\eta}}{N^{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{1-C}{2}\Bigr],\;}
\label{eq:full-formula}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
with the substitutions
$\xistar=2/3$, $\Finf=9/10$, $\delta=+2$,
$\beta = 2+\Finf\xistar = 13/5$,
$\eta_{\infty}=1/2$, and
$c_{\eta} = (\xistar+\delta)/(\Finf+\beta) = 16/21$.
Equivalently, all rationals can be written in terms of $\xistar$,
$\Finf$, $\delta$ alone:
\begin{equation*}
\frac{m^{2}}{\sigzero}
\,=\,
2\pi e\xistar\,\Finf^{2}(1{+}\tfrac{1}{N^{2}})^{2}
\Bigl[\xistar\bigl(\tfrac{J(J{+}1)}{3}{+}\delta\delta_{J,1}\bigr)
\!+\!(\Finf\xistar{+}\delta{-}P)(\mathrm{ex}{+}\xistar)\Bigr]\!
\Bigl[1{+}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}{-}\tfrac{\xistar+\delta}{(\Finf+\Finf\xistar+\delta)N^{2}}\bigr)\!\tfrac{1{-}C}{2}\Bigr].
\end{equation*}

\section{Cross-$N$ empirical verification}\label{sec:verify}

We test the closed form \eqref{eq:full-formula} against the
Athenodorou--Teper $N$-scan \cite{AthenodorouTeper2021}, which covers
$\mathrm{SU}(N)$ for $N \in \{3,4,5,6,8\}$ on a total of $\sim 83$
channels grouped by $(J,P,C,\mathrm{ex})$. After excluding $5$
channels of $2^{+-}$ type that are ditorelon-dominated (multi-glueball
scattering states, see (L3) below), we are left with $78$ channels.

\section{Honest limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{(L1) Absolute scale.} $\sqrt{\sigzero}$ enters as a
dimensional input via QCD transmutation. We do not predict it from
arithmetic; the no-go theorems of \cite{RemondiereNoGo2026} (in
particular T1 of that work) make this explicit.

\textbf{(L2) $14\%$ residual.} The mean absolute residual on $78$
filtered channels is $\sim 14\%$. This is comparable to the published
AT2021 systematic-plus-statistical errors on individual masses but is
by no means competitive with a precision lattice calculation.
Lüscher finite-volume corrections \cite{Luscher1986a,Luscher1986b}
recover at best $5\%$ further on our data.

\textbf{(L3) The $2^{+-}$ channels.} Five $2^{+-}$ channels are
systematically over-predicted by $50$--$60\%$ in the closed form.
These are known to be ditorelon-dominated multi-glueball scattering
states \cite{AthenodorouTeper2021}, mixing into the lowest
single-particle eigenvalue from above. The formula
\eqref{eq:full-formula} is restricted to channels not contaminated by
such low-lying scattering states.

\textbf{(L4) Existence of the mass gap.} This Letter does not address
the Wightman cluster-decomposition proof of $m_{\text{gap}}>0$ in the
continuum: we work with the lattice spectrum and derive the
dimensionless ratio. The existence layer is addressed elsewhere.

\textbf{(L5) Lean status.} Only $K$, $\Finf$, and $\xistar$ are
Lean-proved; the algebraic identities \eqref{eq:beta-derived} and
\eqref{eq:c-eta-derived} are exact rational arithmetic and can be
mechanized straightforwardly, but the spectral factor $c^{2}$ itself
remains empirical at \textsc{Tier~3} (supported, $14\%$).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have written down a closed form for the mass-gap ratio
$m^{2}/\sigzero$ of 4D pure $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ Yang--Mills in every
$J^{PC}(\mathrm{ex})$ channel at every $N\geq 3$, in which every
rational coefficient is generated from three geometric anchors
($\xistar=2/3$, $\Finf=9/10$, $\delta=+2$). The two rationals that
the v1 draft left empirical ($\beta$ and $c_{\eta}$) emerge as exact
algebraic identities \eqref{eq:beta-derived}--\eqref{eq:c-eta-derived}
in the anchors. The $N\to\infty$ limit has zero free parameters.

The roadmap is: (i) independent $\mathrm{Sp}(2N)$ tests
\cite{Bennett2021} of $\eta(N)$ at the same two derived rationals;
(ii) Lean~4 mechanization of \eqref{eq:beta-derived} and
\eqref{eq:c-eta-derived} on top of the existing $K$, $\Finf$,
$\xistar$ kernel; (iii) an arithmetic proof of $\delta=+2$ from a
flux-tube flexion-vibration partition.
