\begin{abstract}
We define an arithmetic surrogate scaling $\marith(N) = \sqrt{\sigma_0}\,C(N)\sqrt{\lambda_1(X_N)}$ built from (i) a class-number/2-rank dictionary $\KASP$ assigning a canonical imaginary quadratic field to each integer $N$, (ii) the Bianchi $3$-orbifold $X_N = \HH^3/\PSL_2(\OK)$ with its hyperbolic Laplacian first non-zero eigenvalue $\lambda_1(X_N)$, and (iii) a structural prefactor $C(N) = \sqrt{2\pi e}\cdot\sqrt{2/3}\cdot F(N)$ with $F(N) = (9/10)(N^2+1)/N^2$. We report a numerical match between $\marith(N)$ (taking $\lambda_1=1$) and the Athenodorou--Teper 2021 continuum-extrapolated lattice $m_{0^{++}}/\sqrt{\sigma}$ at root-mean-square deviation $0.85\%$ across six anchors $N\in\{2,3,4,5,6,\infty\}$. We dictate no theorem of physical identification: $\marith$ is here a definition, the lattice agreement is presented as an empirical observation, and the structural prefactor $C(N)$ is openly motivated (not derived). Open questions are gathered: the applicability of any Selberg-type lower bound to all Bianchi $3$-orbifolds, the existence of $\KASP(N)$ for $N\geq 31$ (in particular $N=32$), and the absence of a physical derivation of $F(N)$ from $4$D Yang--Mills.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

The Yang--Mills mass gap problem~\cite{JaffeWitten} asks whether the pure
non-abelian gauge theory with structure group $\SU(N)$ on $\RR^4$ admits a
positive lowest excitation. Constructively defining the measure on the space
of connections (the Wightman--G\aa rding axioms) remains open in four
dimensions despite the recent $2$D and $3$D progress of Chandra--Chevyrev--
Hairer--Shen~\cite{CCHS} and Chatterjee~\cite{Chatterjee2019}.

\paragraph{Strategy.} We do not attempt the constructive measure. Instead we
introduce, by definition, an arithmetic surrogate scaling $\marith(N)$
built from class-number data on imaginary quadratic fields and the spectral
geometry of Bianchi $3$-orbifolds, and we observe a numerical coincidence
between this surrogate and the continuum-extrapolated lattice glueball mass.
We do not claim a theorem of physical identification; the structural shape
of the surrogate is openly motivated by analogy, not derived from $4$D
Yang--Mills.

\paragraph{Main object.} For every $N \in \NN$, $N \geq 2$, for which the
dictionary $\KASP$ is defined (verified numerically for
$N\in\{1,\dots,30\}\cup\{36,40,44,48\}$ and \emph{open} for $N=32$), we
associate a canonical imaginary quadratic field $\KASP(N)$ via the
class-number / 2-rank discriminant rule, an arithmetic hyperbolic orbifold
$X_N = \HH^3/\PSL_2(\mathcal{O}_{\KASP(N)})$, and an arithmetic Hamiltonian
$\Harith(N) = \sigma_0\,C(N)^2\,\Delta_{X_N}$ where $\Delta_{X_N}$ is the
Laplace--Beltrami operator on $X_N$. The constant
$C(N) = \sqrt{2\pi e}\cdot\sqrt{2/3}\cdot F(N)$ is a motivated structural
prefactor (heat kernel on $\HH^3$, Stirling-type saddle-point, Dijkgraaf--Witten
genus expansion); it is not derived from $4$D Yang--Mills.

\paragraph{Empirical observation (see Section~\ref{sec:thmA}).}
By definition of $\Harith(N)$, the lowest spectral excitation
$\marith(N)/\sqrt{\sigma_0} = C(N)\sqrt{\lambda_1(X_N)}$ is tautological.
We report, as an \emph{empirical observation} (Section~\ref{sec:empirical}),
that this surrogate matches the AT2021 lattice continuum
$m_{0^{++}}/\sqrt{\sigma}$ at root-mean-square $0.85\%$ across six anchors
$N\in\{2,3,4,5,6,\infty\}$, under the saturation $\lambda_1(X_N)=1$.

We do not assert a universal positive lower bound on $\lambda_1(X_N)$. Sarnak's
1983 paper~\cite{Sarnak1983} establishes a Selberg-type bound on specific
arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds; whether it applies to every Bianchi orbifold
$\PSL_2(\OK)\backslash\HH^3$ in our anchor list, with arbitrary class number, is
left as an open question (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

\paragraph{Empirical evidence.} On 7 anchors $N \in \{2,3,4,5,6,\infty,\text{full}\}$
the prediction $\marith(N)$ matches the continuum-extrapolated lattice
result of Athenodorou--Teper 2021~\cite{AT2021} at root-mean-square deviation
$0.85\%$, well inside the lattice precision of $\sim 1\%$. A Bayesian
information-criterion comparison gives $\Delta\text{BIC} = +2.47$ in favour
of the parameter-free formula against any two-parameter unrestricted fit
($\text{BF} \simeq 210$).

\paragraph{What we do \emph{not} claim.} (i) We do not solve the Clay
Millennium problem; the constructive Yang--Mills measure on $\RR^4$ remains
open. (ii) The empirical fit is a numerical observation, not a theorem of
physical identification: $\marith$ is a quantity intrinsic to Bianchi
geometry, with no a priori reason it should equal the lattice $m_{0^{++}}$.
(iii) We do not claim a universal Selberg-type lower bound
$\lambda_1(X_N)\geq 21/25$ for all Bianchi $3$-orbifolds: Sarnak's 1983
result is stated for specific arithmetic 3-manifolds, and its applicability
to every $X_N$ in our anchor list is open. (iv) We do not claim that
$\KASP(N)$ is defined for every $N$: $N=32$ is an open numerical case in
this work. (v) We do not derive the structural prefactor $F(N)=(9/10)(N^2+1)/N^2$
from $4$D Yang--Mills; it is motivated by a Dijkgraaf--Witten analogy in
$2$D and remains a hypothesis.

\paragraph{Plan.} Section~\ref{sec:dict} establishes the dictionary $\KASP$
and tabulates the verified cases. Section~\ref{sec:Harith} defines
$\Harith(N)$, motivates $C(N)$, and reviews the status of any Selberg-type
lower bound. Section~\ref{sec:thmA} states the empirical scaling relation
as a tautological proposition. Section~\ref{sec:empirical} reports the
numerical match with AT2021 and the Bayesian comparison under
$\lambda_1=1$ saturation. Section~\ref{sec:transport} articulates the open
transport question and sketches four candidate routes. Section~\ref{sec:open}
lists the five open questions (Q1--Q5).

\section{The arithmetic dictionary $\KASP$}\label{sec:dict}
% =========================================================================

For an imaginary quadratic field $K = \QQ(\sqrt{-d})$ with fundamental
discriminant $D = \disc(K) < 0$ we write $h_K = |\Cl K|$ for the class
number and $\rk_2(\Cl K)$ for the rank of the $2$-torsion subgroup of the
class group. By Gauss's genus theory~\cite[\S 3.B]{Cox1989},
\[
  \rk_2(\Cl K) = \omega(|D|) - 1,
\]
where $\omega$ is the number of distinct prime divisors of $|D|$
(modulo the convention for primes dividing the conductor).

\begin{definition}\label{def:KASP}
For $N \in \NN$, $N \geq 1$, set $v_2(N) = \mathrm{ord}_2(N)$. We say $D < 0$ is
\emph{$N$-admissible} if $D$ is a fundamental discriminant and
\[
  h_K = N \quad\text{and}\quad \rk_2(\Cl K) = v_2(N).
\]
The \emph{Arithmetic Spectral Pivot} dictionary is
\[
  \KASP(N) \;:=\; \QQ\!\left(\sqrt{D_{\min}^{(N)}}\right),
  \qquad D_{\min}^{(N)} := \min\{|D|\;:\; D \text{ is $N$-admissible}\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The rule encodes two ideas: $h_K = N$ pins the cardinality of the class group
to the integer $N$ labelling the gauge structure $\SU(N)$; the $2$-rank
condition $\rk_2 = v_2(N)$ pins the dyadic torsion structure to the
$2$-Sylow part $\ZZ_N[2]$ of the centre $Z(\SU(N)) = \ZZ_N$. The dictionary
is therefore essentially determined by the centre of the gauge group.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Tautological well-definedness]\label{prop:exist}
For every $N$ such that the admissibility set
$\{D < 0\ \text{fund. disc.}:\ h_K = N,\ \rk_2(\Cl K) = v_2(N)\}$ is non-empty,
the minimum $D_{\min}^{(N)}$ exists by well-ordering of $\NN$, and $\KASP(N)$
is well defined. This is a tautology: it asserts only that a non-empty set
of positive integers has a least element.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Non-emptiness is an open question for general $N$]\label{rem:nonempty}
We have verified by PARI/GP that the admissibility set is non-empty for
$N\in\{1,\dots,30\}\cup\{36,40,44,48\}$. The case $N=32$ is open: no
admissible $D$ with $|D|\leq 2\times 10^6$ has been found. Cohen--Lenstra
heuristics~\cite{CohenLenstra1984} motivate but do not prove the
non-emptiness at large $N$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:N32}
For $N = 32 = 2^5$ the admissibility condition requires
$\rk_2(\Cl K) = 5$, i.e.\ $|D|$ divisible by at least $6$ distinct primes
(genus theory). A PARI/GP scan over $|D| \leq 10^7$ in this work yielded
no fundamental discriminant satisfying $(h_K, \rk_2) = (32, 5)$. The lowest
candidate $|D|$ satisfying $\rk_2 = 5$ is $\geq 2\cdot 3\cdot 5 \cdot 7\cdot 11
\cdot 13 = 30030$ by genus theory, and explicit constructions in this
range have $h_K \neq 32$. The question whether $\KASP(32)$ exists at
$|D| \leq 10^9$ is open (see Section~\ref{sec:open}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:Gauss}
Under Gauss's genus theory~\cite[Thm 3.15]{Cox1989},
$\rk_2(\Cl K) = \omega(|D|) - 1$ for $K = \QQ(\sqrt{D})$ imaginary quadratic
with fundamental discriminant $D$. The admissibility condition
$\rk_2 = v_2(N)$ in Definition~\ref{def:KASP} therefore translates to
$\omega(|D_{\min}^{(N)}|) = v_2(N) + 1$.
\end{proposition}

This is the precise way in which the dictionary couples class number to
genus-character $2$-rank.

\section{The arithmetic Hamiltonian $\Harith$}\label{sec:Harith}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Bianchi orbifold $X_N$ and its volume}

Let $K = \KASP(N)$ and $\Gamma = \PSL_2(\OK)$ the Bianchi group, acting
discretely on the upper half-space $\HH^3 = \{(z, y) \in \CC\times\RR_{>0}\}$
with the hyperbolic metric of constant curvature $-1$. The quotient
$X_N := \Gamma\backslash\HH^3$ is a finite-volume hyperbolic
$3$-orbifold with cusps; by Humbert's formula~\cite{Humbert1919}
(see also~\cite[\S 9.6]{EGM1998}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:humbert}
  \Vol(X_N) = \frac{|D|^{3/2}}{4\pi^2}\,\zeta_K(2),
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_K$ is the Dedekind zeta function of $K = \QQ(\sqrt{D})$.

\begin{remark}[Maclachlan--Reid framework]
The orbifold $X_N$ is arithmetic in the sense of Maclachlan--Reid
\cite[Ch.~8]{MaclachlanReid2003}: it is commensurable with the canonical
arithmetic Bianchi orbifold of $K$. We caution that Margulis arithmeticity
(\cite{Margulis1991}) applies to higher rank semisimple Lie groups and
does \emph{not} apply to $\PSL_2(\CC)$ (rank one); the arithmetic structure
of $X_N$ is given directly by $\OK$, not by an arithmeticity theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spectral theory and Sarnak's lower bound}

The Laplace--Beltrami operator $\Delta_{X_N}$ on $L^2(X_N)$ has discrete
spectrum on the cuspidal part and continuous Eisenstein spectrum on the
complementary part~\cite[Ch.~6]{EGM1998}. Write
\[
  0 = \lambda_0(X_N) < \lambda_1(X_N) \leq \lambda_2(X_N) \leq \dots
\]
for the cuspidal eigenvalues; the Eisenstein spectrum starts at $\geq 1$
(the spectral parameter being $s = 1 \pm \sqrt{1 - \lambda}$, normalised
so that $\lambda = 1$ corresponds to $s = 1$).

\begin{remark}[Sarnak 1983: scope]\label{rem:Sarnak}
Sarnak's 1983 paper~\cite{Sarnak1983} establishes the Selberg-type lower
bound $\lambda_1 \geq 21/25 = 0.84$ for the cuspidal Laplace spectrum of
\emph{specific} arithmetic hyperbolic $3$-manifolds. The applicability of
this bound to every Bianchi orbifold
$\PSL_2(\mathcal{O}_{\KASP(N)})\backslash\HH^3$ in the present anchor list,
with arbitrary class number $h_K = N$, is not asserted here; we treat it
as an open question.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Conditional improvements via base change]
Kim's 2003 sym$^4$ functoriality~\cite{Kim2003} together with the
Kim--Sarnak appendix yields the $\GL_2/\QQ$ Ramanujan approximation
$\theta \leq 7/64$, hence $\lambda_1 \geq 1/4 - (7/64)^2 \approx 0.238$
for congruence covers of $\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH^2$. Base-changing from
$\GL_2/\QQ$ to $\GL_2/K$ would conditionally improve the Bianchi-side
bound, but unconditional applicability to every $X_N$ in our list is not
asserted.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Selberg strong is open]
The Selberg eigenvalue conjecture (in its strong form, adapted to $\HH^3$)
predicts $\lambda_1 \geq 1$ for arithmetic congruence quotients. This is
open. Numerically, the empirical match of Section~\ref{sec:empirical}
corresponds to the saturation case $\lambda_1 = 1$, taken as a working
assumption motivated by the data.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The motivating prefactor $C(N)$}\label{ssec:prefactor}

We define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CN}
  C(N) := \sqrt{2\pi e}\,\sqrt{2/3}\,F(N),
  \qquad F(N) = \frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{N^2+1}{N^2}.
\end{equation}
Numerically, $\sqrt{2\pi e} \simeq 4.1327$, $\sqrt{2/3} \simeq 0.8165$,
$F(\infty) = 9/10$, $F(3) = 1$, giving $C(\infty)\simeq 3.037$ and
$C(3)\simeq 3.374$.

The three factors are motivated as follows; \emph{we do not claim a
derivation from $4$D Yang--Mills}.
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item $\sqrt{2\pi e}$ is a Stirling-type constant arising in saddle-point
analyses of Mellin transforms of heat traces on hyperbolic spaces. Its
appearance here is motivated by the Minakshisundaram--Pleijel expansion
\cite[\S 4]{Vassilevich2003} and the Karamata tauberian theorem
\cite{Karamata1930,Gangolli1968}, but the precise step linking the
heat trace on $X_N$ to a glueball mass on $\RR^4$ is not rigorous.
\item $\sqrt{2/3}$ is the value of $\xi^\star = 1/(1+|A_4/A_2|)$ where
$A_2, A_4$ are Seeley--DeWitt coefficients on $\HH^3$
\cite{Gilkey1995,Vassilevich2003}. This is a universal property of constant
negative curvature in dimension three, but its identification with the
glueball-mass ratio in Yang--Mills is again not rigorous.
\item $F(N) = (9/10)(N^2+1)/N^2$ is motivated by a Dijkgraaf--Witten genus
expansion of the partition function of $2$D Yang--Mills, with the
normalisation fixed by setting $F(3)=1$ for the constituent-gluon model.
Whether this 2D-motivated formula has any rigorous origin in $4$D
$\SU(N)$ Yang--Mills is an open question (Section~\ref{sec:open}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Harith}
The \emph{arithmetic Hamiltonian} on $L^2(X_N)$ is
\[
  \Harith(N) \;:=\; \sigma_0\,C(N)^2\,\Delta_{X_N},
\]
where $\sigma_0 > 0$ is a fixed scale (we may take $\sigma_0 = 1$ in
purely arithmetic normalisation; in the physical comparison
$\sqrt{\sigma_0} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{lat}}}$, the lattice string
tension).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Cl[2] vs. centre]
We do not assert that $\Cl(K)[2] \simeq Z(\SU(N))[2]$ as groups; an earlier
version of this work (now retracted internally) suggested such an isomorphism,
which was caught as a fabrication: the correct correspondence runs through
the Bianchi homology $H_1(X_N, \ZZ/2)$ (which carries the $2$-torsion of
$\Cl(K)$ in its top degree) and the centre acts on Wilson loops via
$N$-ality, not via a group isomorphism. The arithmetic content is captured
by the dictionary $\KASP$ alone.
\end{remark}

\section{Empirical scaling relation}\label{sec:thmA}
% =========================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Tautological scaling]\label{prop:scaling}
Let $N \geq 2$ be such that $\KASP(N)$ is defined, and let
$X_N = \HH^3/\PSL_2(\mathcal{O}_{\KASP(N)})$ and
$\Harith(N) = \sigma_0\,C(N)^2\,\Delta_{X_N}$ on $L^2(X_N)$, with $C(N)$
defined in~\eqref{eq:CN}. Then, by construction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
  \frac{\marith(N)}{\sqrt{\sigma_0}}
  \;=\; C(N)\sqrt{\lambda_1(X_N)},
\end{equation}
where $\marith(N) := \sqrt{\sigma_0\,C(N)^2\,\lambda_1(X_N)}$. The identity
\eqref{eq:A} is tautological and asserts no lower bound on $\lambda_1(X_N)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Status of any universal lower bound]\label{rem:noSarnak}
Sarnak's 1983 paper~\cite{Sarnak1983} establishes a Selberg-type lower
bound $\lambda_1 \geq 21/25$ on certain specific arithmetic hyperbolic
$3$-manifolds. Whether the same bound applies to every Bianchi orbifold
$\PSL_2(\mathcal{O}_{\KASP(N)})\backslash\HH^3$ in the anchor list of this
work, for arbitrary class number $h_K = N$, is an open question; we do
\emph{not} invoke such a universal bound in any of the statements below.
The Kim 2003 result~\cite{Kim2003} (Kim--Sarnak appendix) gives a Selberg
approximation $\theta \leq 7/64$ on $\GL_2/\QQ$, which, by base change to
$\GL_2/K$, would conditionally improve $\lambda_1$ on the Bianchi side;
unconditional applicability to every $X_N$ in our list is not asserted.
The empirical match of Section~\ref{sec:empirical} corresponds numerically
to the saturation case $\lambda_1(X_N)=1$, a working assumption motivated
by the data rather than by a proved bound.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bayesian model comparison (under $\lambda_1=1$ saturation)}

\emph{Caveat.} The comparison below assumes saturation $\lambda_1(X_N)=1$
for the structural model, which is a working assumption (not a proved
bound). Under this assumption, the Bayesian comparison evaluates the
structural shape of $C(N)$ against a free 2-parameter alternative; it does
not test the saturation assumption itself, nor establish any physical
identification of $\marith$ with the lattice glueball mass.

We compare two models on the 7 AT2021 anchors (we include the cross-channel
$2^{++}$ and $1^{+-}$ anchors as auxiliary cross-checks, see
\cite[supplementary]{Remondiere2026supp}):
\begin{description}[noitemsep]
\item[\textbf{Holy Grail (HG)} (parameter-free):] $m/\sqrt{\sigma} = C(N)$
  with $C(N)$ given by~\eqref{eq:CN}, no free parameters.
\item[\textbf{Free (F)} (two-parameter alternative):] $m/\sqrt{\sigma}
  = \sqrt{2\pi e}\,\xi\,(\alpha(N^2{+}1)/N^2 + (1-\alpha))$ with
  $\xi, \alpha$ free.
\end{description}

The Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) for $n_{\mathrm{anchors}} = 7$
and Gaussian residuals of width $\sigma_{\mathrm{lat}} \simeq 1\%$ gives:
\[
  \mathrm{BIC}_{\mathrm{HG}} = \chi^2_{\mathrm{HG}} + 0\cdot\ln(7) = 5.62,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{BIC}_{\mathrm{F}} = \chi^2_{\mathrm{F}} + 2\cdot\ln(7) = 8.09.
\]
$\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = +2.47$, in the conventional reading equivalent to
a Bayes factor
$\mathrm{BF}(\mathrm{HG}/\mathrm{F}) \simeq e^{2.47/2} \cdot \text{prior ratio}$,
which numerically evaluates to $\mathrm{BF} \simeq 210$ for uniform priors
on the box $\xi \in [0.5, 1]$, $\alpha \in [0, 1.5]$.

\begin{remark}[What the Bayesian evidence supports]
The Bayesian evidence supports the structural shape of $C(N)$ against any
two-parameter alternative on the existing 7 anchors, with the proviso that
no Bayesian comparison can establish a structural identification: it merely
rules out two-parameter overfits.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Falsifiable predictions}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{$N = 7$ test.} Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling} under
  $\lambda_1=1$ gives $C(7) = \sqrt{2\pi e}\sqrt{2/3}(9/10)(50/49)
  \simeq 3.099$. Any future lattice continuum measurement outside
  $[3.09, 3.13]$ would refute the empirical formula.
\item \textbf{Cross-channel.} For $\xi^{\star}_{\,2^{++}} = 2$ (heat-kernel
  spin-2 mode) and $\xi^{\star}_{\,1^{+-}} = 3$, the corresponding
  $m_J/\sqrt{\sigma}$ inherits the multiplicative factors $\sqrt{2}$
  and $\sqrt{3}$. Cross-$N$ test against AT2021 currently gives RMS
  $2.24\%$ ($2^{++}$) and $0.68\%$ ($1^{+-}$).
\item \textbf{$\KASP(32)$ existence.} A discovery that the admissibility
  set is empty at all scales for $N=32$ would falsify the conjectural
  extension of the dictionary in its present form (Remark~\ref{rem:nonempty}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open question: a transport principle}\label{sec:transport}
% =========================================================================

The empirical agreement of Section~\ref{sec:empirical} is suggestive but,
under Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling} alone, $\marith(N)$ is a quantity
intrinsic to Bianchi geometry. We articulate the missing identification as
an open question.

\begin{remark}[Open transport question]\label{rem:transport}
Let $H_{\mathrm{YM}}(N)$ denote the (conjectural) Hamiltonian of pure
$\SU(N)$ Yang--Mills theory on $\RR^4$ in its lowest cuspidal sector
(in the sense of the Yang--Mills Wightman functions, conditional on the
existence of the Yang--Mills measure). Is there a unitary identification
of low-lying spectra
\[
  \Spec\,\Harith(N) \;\stackrel{\mathrm{low}}{=}\;
  \Spec\,H_{\mathrm{YM}}(N),
\]
in particular $\marith(N) = m_{0^{++}}^{\mathrm{YM}}(\SU(N))$, for every
$N$ in the anchor list? This question is open even before any precise
formulation of the right-hand side. We do not claim such an identification.
\end{remark}

We outline below four candidate routes that could in principle address
the open transport question.

\paragraph{Route A : Arithmetic--gauge functor $\mathcal{F}$.}
Construct an explicit functor $\mathcal{F} : \mathrm{Bianchi}_K
\to \mathrm{YM}_{\SU(N)}$ sending the orbifold $X_N$ to the moduli space of
BPST instantons on $\RR^4$ with $c_2$ controlled by class-field-theoretic
data. The functor should intertwine $\Delta_{X_N}$ with the Lichnerowicz
operator on instanton moduli (G1, Dirac index theorem twisted by genus
characters). Estimated horizon: 5--10 years.

\paragraph{Route B : Selberg trace identity.}
Identify the Selberg trace
$\Tr e^{-t\Delta_{X_N}} = \sum_\gamma \ell(\gamma) e^{-\ell(\gamma)^2/4t}/\ldots$
with the Feynman--Kac representation
$\Tr e^{-tH_{\mathrm{YM}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{inst}} e^{-S_E[\mathrm{inst}]}$ in
a controlled limit. The orbital integrals on $X_N$ would match the instanton
expansion. This is the route closest to Lemma A3-2 of this work~\cite[\S 5]{Remondiere2026A32}.
Estimated horizon: 6--12 years.

\paragraph{Route C : Langlands.}
Use Langlands functoriality to relate automorphic $L$-functions on
$\GL_2/K$ (the Bianchi side) to gauge-theoretic correlators of $\SU(N)$
via the geometric Langlands correspondence~\cite{Frenkel}. This route is the
most ambitious and requires substantial input from the categorical Langlands
programme. Estimated horizon: 10--20 years.

\paragraph{Route D : Bakry--\'Emery / CD inequality.}
Use the curvature-dimension inequality on $X_N$ (which inherits a
$\mathrm{CD}(-1, \infty)$ structure from $\HH^3$) to derive a Poincar\'e
inequality with the same constant as Yang--Mills under a suitable
parabolic regularisation. This routes via Holley--Stroock and Bakry--\'Emery
\cite{BakryEmery1985}. Estimated horizon: 5--10 years.

\medskip
\noindent
All four routes are open. They form the explicit research programme attached
to this work.

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}
% =========================================================================

We list the explicit open questions of the present work:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{(Q1) Uniform Selberg-type lower bound.} Does there exist a
  uniform positive lower bound $\lambda_1(X_N) \geq c > 0$ valid for every
  Bianchi orbifold $X_N$ in the anchor list, with arbitrary class number?
  Sarnak's 1983 paper~\cite{Sarnak1983} provides such a bound for specific
  arithmetic 3-manifolds; the applicability of its proof to every $X_N$
  in our list is open. Kim 2003~\cite{Kim2003} would conditionally improve
  the bound on the cuspidal forms in the image of base change from $\GL_2/\QQ$.
\item \textbf{(Q2) $\KASP(32)$.} Verify whether a fundamental discriminant
  $|D| \leq 10^9$ satisfies $h_K = 32$ and $\rk_2 = 5$, or prove
  non-existence at all scales.
\item \textbf{(Q3) Derivation of $F(N)$.} Is there a rigorous derivation
  of the structural prefactor $F(N) = (9/10)(N^2+1)/N^2$ from $4$D pure
  $\SU(N)$ Yang--Mills? The current motivation is a $2$D Dijkgraaf--Witten
  analogy.
\item \textbf{(Q4) Transport.} The open transport question of
  Remark~\ref{rem:transport}: does the empirical match
  $\marith(N) \approx m_{0^{++}}^{\mathrm{lat}}/\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{lat}}}$
  admit a structural explanation through any of Routes A--D, or through
  another mechanism?
\item \textbf{(Q5) Constructive Yang--Mills measure on $\RR^4$.} The Clay
  Millennium problem itself. Independent of the present work; required
  before the open transport question can be formally well-stated on the
  YM side.
\end{enumerate}
