# A scalar finite-volume criterion for a continuum mass gap in SU(2) lattice gauge theory

Version 1.2 (12 November 2026): adds the reverse implication (Theorem R, Section 4) with a second Lean file, rewrites the comparison with version 5.0.0 of Bhattacharjee et al., and adds an example showing that positivity of the transfer operator cannot be dropped. Version 1.1 is the Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.24152406 (literature corrections only) and version 1.0 the record 10.5281/zenodo.24150807 (concept 24150806). `lean/CritereScalaire.lean` and `lean/main.lean` are byte for byte those of version 1.0. Author: Kévin Rémondière (ORCID [0009-0008-2443-7166](https://orcid.org/0009-0008-2443-7166)).

A technical note that transposes the two-dimensional finite-volume doubling criterion of OpenAI (Proposition 2.2 of *Sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional O(4) model*, 23 September 2026) to SU(2) lattice gauge theories in dimensions four and three, with a conditional continuum assembly and a converse. It is predominantly a transposition, not a new mass-gap mechanism.

## What the note says, and does not say
- Under symmetry and a positive trace-class transfer representation (hypothesis, not formalised), `Δ(n) = 16 log Z(n⁴) − log Z((2n)⁴) ≤ 2⁻⁹` implies `Δ(2n) ≤ 256 Δ(n)²` and `(λᵢ/λ₀)ⁿ ≤ 256 Δ(n) ≤ ½` on every spatial torus `(2ʲn)³` (dimension 3: `2⁻⁷`, 64).
- If `Δ_{β_k}(n_k) ≤ η ≤ 2⁻⁹` along lattices of spacing `ℓ/n_k` and the lattice Osterwalder–Schrader forms converge (existence of the continuum limit: **assumed**), the limit has the q-form of the gap with `q = 256η`; under OS reconstruction (**assumed**) a mass gap `≥ (log 2)/ℓ` and a unique vacuum. This implication is formalised in Lean 4 against the author's statement ClayYM v1.1, with named hypotheses.
- A converse (finite-volume gap with flux sectors, plus a trace bound) is proved on paper at fixed physical size.
- **Not claimed**: any estimate of `Δ` at weak coupling; existence or non-triviality of the continuum limit; reconstruction; application to the formalised construction of OpenAI (Section 10); equivalence with the Yang–Mills mass-gap problem. The Rényi-2 reading of `Δ` is a reformulation. The strong-coupling instance (`β ≤ 0.005`) is conditional on computational inputs reported from one program. The Maxwell witness is conditional on a model identification not derived; the massive-scalar thresholds are numerical.
- **What version 1.2 changes**: Section 4 (Theorem R): under symmetry and a positive trace-class transfer representation, `Δ(n) ≤ 2⁻⁹` implies the one-scale hypothesis (B*) of Bhattacharjee et al. v5.0.0 at the scale `16n` (explicit `γ = 1/(31·2²³T0³)`, `2γ(96T0)³ = 27/3968 < 1/20`), a gap `≥ (log 2)/n` on every integer cube of side `N ≥ n`, and `≥ 0.2393/n` on tori of aspect ratio ≤ 24; together with the implication (B*) ⇒ `Δ ≤ 2⁻⁹` already in v1.1, the two criteria are equivalent up to a change of scale (16 in the reverse direction, 32 for a round trip; 16 is not optimal; the optimal factor is not known). Positivity of the transfer operator is indispensable (Remark in Section 4, with an example where `Δ < 0`). Corollary (bridge to their Theorem F) is **conditional on their hypothesis (W) and their Theorem F as we read them; not verified by us**. This does **not** answer a question left open in their text (their datum is a gap plus a bound on one box, not our `Δ ≤ 2⁻⁹`) and is **not** a proof of the mass gap. - **What version 1.1 changed** (Section 1.3, the comparison of Section 8.2, the bibliography, the Use-of-AI section, the status statements): the principle "an inequality between partition functions of finite periodic tori, twisted against untwisted, implies decay" is recorded as classical (Tomboulis–Yaffe 1985; Kovács–Tomboulis 2002, eq. (11); Kanazawa 2008, Theorems 1, 2, 5), and a comparison item is added (same family of argument; different hypothesis: insensitivity to a centre twist, not the doubling defect; different conclusion: Wilson loops or spin correlations of a periodic box, not a bound on the transfer matrix of every spatial torus). Nothing in `lean/`, `ClayYM_v1_1_extrait/`, `reproduction/` or `rejeu_depuis_arbre_git_2026-11-12/` changes.
- **Relation to earlier public work**: version 5.0.0 of the series of Bhattacharjee et al. (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24147967; record created on 11 November 2026, before this note was released; read by us after the note was written) contains, for four-dimensional tori, the same doubling defect, its bracket by thermal traces, the purity reading, and a one-scale criterion whose hypothesis (a family of inequalities between partition functions of finite tori) implies ours and whose conclusion is wider. For the Wilson theory in dimension four the lattice part of this note is a variant of their criterion with a weaker hypothesis and a narrower conclusion (dyadic cubes only). No priority is claimed (Section 1.4 of the note, rewritten in v1.2).
