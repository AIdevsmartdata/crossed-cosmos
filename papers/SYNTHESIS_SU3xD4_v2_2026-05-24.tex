\subsection{Mathematical anthropy via the 5-condition uniqueness theorem}

The classical anthropic principle is \emph{post hoc} selection: ``of all possible $(G, D)$, we live in one where observers can exist'' (Carter 2006, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0606117}{gr-qc/0606117}). Tegmark (\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9702052}{gr-qc/9702052}) sharpens this by arguing $D = 4$ is the unique dimension in which (i) hyperbolic field equations are well-posed, (ii) atoms are stable, (iii) gravity is non-trivial. The argument is physical but not mathematical: it requires \emph{some} observer-like substructure.

Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness} shifts the framing. The 5-condition uniqueness --- saturation, maximal degeneracy, $D \geq 3$, complex Weyl admissibility, EW non-saturation --- is a \emph{purely mathematical} selector. It does not invoke observers, atoms, or hyperbolicity. It says: \emph{among all simple-Lie-group $\times$ dimension pairs, only $(\SU(3) \times \SU(2) \times \Uone, D = 4)$ is internally consistent under (C1)--(C5)}.

We propose this as a \emph{mathematical anthropy} refinement of the classical anthropic principle: the universe does not select $D = 4$ ``because we are in it''; the conditions (C1)--(C5), each independently motivated, uniquely admit the Standard Model gauge group in spacetime dimension four. This is a strengthening, not a replacement, of the classical argument: it shows that the mathematical constraints \emph{alone} --- independent of life or observers --- single out the Standard Model assignment. Carter's and Tegmark's arguments remain valid as physical confirmations of the mathematical selection.
